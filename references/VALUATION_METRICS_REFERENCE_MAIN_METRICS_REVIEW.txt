================================================================================
VALUATION METRICS GUIDE
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MAIN VALUE METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are cross-regime valuation metrics that can remain useful whether earnings / FCF
are positive or negative, as long as the denominator itself is positive and economically
meaningful. They form the first valuation layer before profitability-specific metrics.

METRIC: P/S (Annualized)
DEFINITION: Market capitalization / annualized revenue.
IMPACT: Shows how much equity investors are paying for each dollar of annualized sales. Useful when earnings are negative or too small for P/E to be meaningful.
SCORE: Tier 1 for unprofitable companies / Tier 2 for profitable companies
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.

Suggested ranges:

< 2x: Low / potentially attractive
2x–5x: Moderate
5x–10x: Expensive / growth premium
> 10x: Very expensive / requires strong growth and margins

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Revenue
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / total annualized revenue.
IMPACT: Tells you exactly how much you are paying to buy one dollar of their total sales, adjusting for debt and cash.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Core / Universal Sales Valuation)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 2.0: Deep value
  • 2.0–5.0: Typical / Average
  • > 5.0: Expensive (reserved for hyper-growth tech/software)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Gross Profit
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / annualized gross profit.
IMPACT: Shows if the core product itself is actually profitable to make, before paying for all the corporate overhead and management salaries.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Conditional / Margin Comparison)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Helps normalize expensive EV/Revenue multiples for companies that have incredibly high gross margins.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Revenue / Growth
DEFINITION: (EV / Revenue) / YoY Revenue Growth Rate (%).
IMPACT: Helps justify a high price tag by factoring in how incredibly fast the company's sales are multiplying year over year.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Common Context)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  Functions like a PEG ratio for top-line sales. A lower ratio means you are getting more growth for the price you are paying.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Price to Book (P/B)
DEFINITION: Market cap / total equity (or tangible book value).
IMPACT: Shows if you are paying a fair price for the actual physical "stuff" the company owns (buildings, cash, equipment).
SCORE: Tier 2 (Asset-Heavy / Financial Companies Only)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 1.0: Cheap (trading below liquidation value)
  • 1.0–3.0: Typical
  • > 3.0: Expensive (unless highly profitable)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PROFITABILITY-BASED VALUATION METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These metrics depend on positive or meaningfully normalized earnings, EBITDA, EBIT,
or free cash flow. They should not be forced onto companies with negative denominators.

METRIC: Forward P/E (NTM)
DEFINITION: Current share price / consensus next-twelve-month diluted EPS.
IMPACT: Tells you if you are overpaying today for the company's future success. It focuses on where the business is going rather than where it has been.
looks forward at the Next Twelve Months of expected earnings.
SCORE: Tier 1 (Very High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 15: Cheap / Undervalued
  • 15–25: Typical / Fair
  • > 25: Expensive (priced for high growth)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Trailing P/E
DEFINITION: Market cap / trailing net income, or share price / trailing diluted EPS.
IMPACT: A reality check based on cold, hard facts. It shows exactly how much you are paying for the actual profits the business generated over the last 12 months.
SCORE: Tier 1 (Very High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 15: Cheap / Value territory
  • 15–25: Typical / Average
  • > 25: Expensive / Premium

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / EBITDA
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / EBITDA.
IMPACT: Gives you the total price tag of the entire business compared to its raw earning power. It lets you compare companies regardless of their debt or tax rates.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 8: Cheap
  • 8–14: Typical for established companies
  • > 15: Expensive (implies high growth expectations)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Levered FCF Yield
DEFINITION: FCF available to common equity / market cap.
IMPACT: Shows the true "cash return" you get for buying the stock. It is the actual cash the business puts in its pocket relative to its stock price.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Higher is better.
  • > 10%: Cheap valuation (high yield)
  • 5%–10%: Typical / Average
  • < 5%: Expensive (low yield)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / EBIT
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / operating income.
IMPACT: Similar to EV/EBITDA, but it factors in the very real cost of physical equipment wearing out over time.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Common / Recommended)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Typically runs slightly higher than EV/EBITDA. Lower values indicate you are paying less for core operating profits.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / FCFF (Unlevered FCF)
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / free cash flow to the firm.
IMPACT: Shows the total cash the business generates for everyone involved before any loans are paid off.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Common / Conditional)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 15: Attractive
  • 15–25: Typical
  • > 30: Expensive

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Normalized Multi-Year Multiples
DEFINITION: 3-to-5 year averaged earnings or FCF / Valuation.
IMPACT: Smooths out the roller coaster of unusually good and bad years so you can clearly see the true, long-term trend of the business.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Common)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Ranges depend on the underlying metric (e.g., smoothed P/E should follow standard P/E ranges).

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: PEG Ratio
DEFINITION: P/E ratio / expected EPS growth rate (as a whole number).
IMPACT: Helps you figure out if a fast-growing stock is actually a good deal, or if you are just paying a premium price for market hype.
SCORE: Tier 1 (Optional Context)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 1.0: Undervalued relative to growth
  • 1.0–2.0: Typical for growth stocks
  • > 2.0: Expensive

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: FCF Conversion
DEFINITION: FCF available to equity / net income.
IMPACT: Acts as a lie-detector test for the company's accountants. It verifies that the "profits" reported on paper are actually backed by real cash.
SCORE: Tier 3 (Supporting Check)
VALUE MEASURE: Higher is better.
  • > 100%: Excellent (profits are fully backed by cash)
  • < 100%: Needs monitoring (profits exceed actual cash generated)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Total Shareholder Yield
DEFINITION: (Cash dividends + share repurchases - share issuance) / market cap.
IMPACT: Shows the total slice of the pie the company is handing directly back to you, combining dividends and stock buybacks.
SCORE: Tier 3 (Supporting Check)
VALUE MEASURE: Higher is better.
  • > 5%: High cash return to shareholders
  • 2%–4%: Typical for mature companies
  • < 2%: Low yield

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Price to Owner Earnings
DEFINITION: Market cap / (Net income + D&A - maintenance CapEx ± Δ working capital).
IMPACT: Reveals how much cash the owner can actually pull out of the business without breaking the current day-to-day operations.
SCORE: Tier 4 (Deep Dive Only)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Functions similarly to standard P/E ranges but uses a stricter cash definition.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / NOPAT
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / [EBIT x (1 - normalized operating tax rate)].
IMPACT: A pure look at the company's operating profits after paying taxes, completely ignoring the costs of their debt.
SCORE: Tier 4 (Deep Dive / Filter Out)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Tends to mirror EV/EBIT benchmarks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. VALUATION METRICS WHEN PROFITS / FCF ARE NEGATIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When earnings or FCF are negative, profit-based multiples such as P/E, EV / EBIT,
EV / EBITDA, and FCF-based valuation multiples can become meaningless or misleading.


METRIC: EV / Revenue
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / total annualized revenue.
IMPACT: Tells you exactly how much you are paying to buy one dollar of their total sales, adjusting for debt and cash.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Core for Unprofitable Growth)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  • < 2.0: Deep value
  • 2.0–5.0: Typical / Average
  • > 5.0: Expensive (reserved for hyper-growth tech/software)

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Gross Profit
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / annualized gross profit.
IMPACT: Shows if the core product itself is actually profitable to make, before paying for all the corporate overhead and management salaries.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High for Segment & Margin Expansion)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  Helps normalize expensive EV/Revenue multiples for companies that have incredibly high gross margins.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Backlog
DEFINITION: Enterprise value / total order backlog.
IMPACT: Essential for project-based companies. It measures your investment against the guaranteed future work the company has already signed contracts for.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High for Defense / Aerospace / Project-Based)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better (cheaper).
  A lower number means you are paying less for every dollar of future guaranteed revenue.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: EV / Revenue / Growth
DEFINITION: (EV / Revenue) / YoY Revenue Growth Rate (%).
IMPACT: Helps justify a high price tag by factoring in how incredibly fast the company's sales are multiplying year over year.
SCORE: Tier 2 (Common Context)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  Functions like a PEG ratio for top-line sales. A lower ratio means you are getting more growth for the price you are paying.

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Price to Book (P/B)
DEFINITION: Market cap / total equity (or tangible book value).
IMPACT: Shows if you are paying a fair price for the actual physical "stuff" the company owns (buildings, cash, equipment).
SCORE: Tier 1 (Asset-Heavy / Financial Exceptions)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 1.0: Cheap (trading below liquidation value)
  • 1.0–3.0: Typical
  • > 3.0: Expensive (unless highly profitable)


================================================================================
4. SHORT INTEREST & STOCK-BASED COMPENSATION METRICS
================================================================================
These metrics provide additional risk, dilution, and cash-flow quality context. They are
especially useful for heavily shorted stocks and companies that rely significantly on
stock-based compensation.

METRIC: Short Interest % of Float
DEFINITION: Shares sold short / public float.
IMPACT: Shows how heavily investors are betting against the stock. Very high short interest can signal concerns about valuation, fundamentals, execution, or accounting, while also increasing short-squeeze potential.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Risk Context)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is generally safer.
  • < 5%: Low / Normal
  • 5%–10%: Moderate
  • 10%–20%: High
  • > 20%: Very High / Significant bearish positioning

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Short Ratio / Days to Cover
DEFINITION: Shares sold short / average daily trading volume.
IMPACT: Estimates how many normal trading days it would take short sellers to buy back all shares currently sold short. A high ratio can signal crowded bearish positioning and greater short-squeeze risk.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Risk Context)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower generally indicates less short-side pressure.
  • < 2 days: Low
  • 2–5 days: Normal / Moderate
  • 5–10 days: High
  • > 10 days: Very High / Crowded short

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: SBC / Revenue
DEFINITION: Stock-based compensation expense / revenue.
IMPACT: Shows how much of the company's revenue is being consumed by equity compensation. It is particularly useful for software, technology, and high-growth companies where SBC can be a major recurring expense.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Core SBC Metric)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 5%: Low / Healthy
  • 5%–10%: Moderate
  • 10%–20%: High
  • > 20%: Very High

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: SBC / Free Cash Flow
DEFINITION: Stock-based compensation / reported free cash flow.
IMPACT: Shows how dependent reported FCF is on adding back stock compensation. A high ratio can indicate that reported cash generation overstates the economic benefit reaching shareholders.
SCORE: Tier 2 (High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 20%: Strong
  • 20%–40%: Moderate
  • 40%–75%: High
  • > 75%: Very High / FCF quality concern
  • > 100%: SBC exceeds reported FCF

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: SBC-Adjusted FCF Yield
DEFINITION: (Levered FCF - stock-based compensation) / market capitalization.
IMPACT: Measures shareholder cash yield after treating stock-based compensation as an economic cost. It provides a stricter valuation view than reported FCF Yield for companies with significant SBC.
SCORE: Tier 1 (Very High / Core)
VALUE MEASURE: Higher is better.
  • > 8%: Attractive
  • 4%–8%: Reasonable
  • 2%–4%: Expensive
  • < 2%: Very Expensive

--------------------------------------------------
METRIC: Net Share Dilution
DEFINITION: Percentage change in diluted shares outstanding over the period.
IMPACT: Shows whether stock-based compensation and new share issuance are diluting existing shareholders after accounting for company buybacks.
SCORE: Tier 1 (High / Shareholder Impact)
VALUE MEASURE: Lower is better.
  • < 0%: Share count shrinking / Positive
  • 0%–1%: Minimal dilution
  • 1%–3%: Moderate dilution
  • 3%–5%: High dilution
  • > 5%: Very High dilution
